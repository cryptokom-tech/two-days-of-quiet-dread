# Candidates — Two Days of Quiet Dread

Judge brief (in order): (1) single-line code review comment approving the
PR with quiet existential dread; (2) funny on first read, laugh from the
dread itself; (3) quiet, understatement, flat tone; (4) reads like a tired
engineer would really paste before clicking approve; (5) fresh turn of
phrase. Max 140 characters, single line. All candidates below are original
— none copies any visible competitor entry or house text.

## Ranked strongest first

1. **C1 — SUBMITTED (86 chars)**
   `Approved. I understand every line of this PR, and that is precisely what frightens me.`
   Inversion: reviewers fear what they don't understand, so understanding
   everything and being frightened by that is the surprise. Flat tone,
   real approval, compact (tie-break favours smaller text).

2. **C2 (102 chars)**
   `LGTM. The tests pass, the types check, and the silence after I click approve will be the loudest part.`
   Quiet dread located in the moment after approval; poetic but still a
   plausible comment. Slightly more literary than C1.

3. **C3 (112 chars)**
   `Approved. In six months someone will git blame this line, find my name, and I will have no memory of writing it.`
   Very relatable truth (forgetting your own code); strong on criterion 4.
   Weaker surprise than C1/C2 — the git-blame angle is familiar.

4. **C4 (106 chars)**
   `LGTM. The diff is clean, the tests are green, and I am already grieving the engineer who will maintain it.`
   Fresh image ("grieving the engineer who will maintain it") but
   "grieving" leans a touch melodramatic for criterion 3.

5. **C5 (107 chars)**
   `Approved. The code works, the tests pass, and I have stopped asking why — that is the part that worries me.`
   Flat and real, but "works but nobody knows why" is a known trope;
   freshness risk.

6. **C7 (90 chars)**
   `Approved. This PR is perfect. That is the most frightening thing I have reviewed all week.`
   Clean inversion, but "perfect" reads slightly bold for a quiet comment.

7. **C8 (128 chars)**
   `LGTM. The diff is small, the tests are green, and in two years a stranger will read this comment and wonder what I was thinking.`
   Too close in spirit to visible field entries about a future reader
   apologising/wondering; freshness risk, and longest of the set.

8. **C6 (92 chars)**
   `LGTM. Merging this on a Friday, because the dread feels quieter when the weekend absorbs it.`
   Friday-merge is a stock trope; weakest on criterion 5.

## Field notes (visible submission titles at build time, ~12:10 WIB 24 Nov)

Common patterns already in the field — avoided as angles: "future me
apologising for this merge", "read the whole diff twice and found nothing
wrong, which somehow makes it worse", "it will outlive us all", "whatever
it becomes after we are gone". C1's inversion (fear *from* understanding)
does not appear among the visible titles.
